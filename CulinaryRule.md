# Culinary Workflow Engineering Rules

These core rules of culinary workflow engineering are designed to balance efficiency, food safety, and maximum food quality when scaling up recipes.

---

### ⏱️ Rule 1: The Timeline Rule (Reverse Engineering)
*   **Work backward:** Start your timeline from the exact minute the food must be served ($00:00$).
*   **Calculate active time:** Add the cooking times of all dishes to determine the exact kitchen start time.

### 🌡️ Rule 2: The Thermal Stability Rule
*   **Cook stable foods first:** Identify dishes that hold temperature well, can be reheated easily, or improve in flavor over time (e.g., soups, stews, braises). Schedule these early.
*   **Plan hold or rest:** Define exactly *how* a dish will stay warm, such as leaving a lid on a heavy stockpot, using a warming drawer, or keeping trays at room temperature.

### ⚡ Rule 3: The Last-Minute (Flash) Rule
*   **Protect textures:** Delicate proteins (seafood), high-heat items (stir-fries), and crispy textures (fried foods) must be cooked at the very final minute.
*   **Complete mise en place:** For last-minute dishes, 100% of ingredients must be cut, measured, and placed within arm's reach of the stove before the heat is turned on.

### 🧺 Rule 4: The Batching Rule
*   **Consolidate prep:** Group similar tasks across different recipes. Chop all onions for the soup, traybake, and sauces at the same time to save washing knives and boards.
*   **Respect pan capacity:** Never crowd a pan when cooking for large groups. If a recipe is too large for the wok, split it into smaller high-heat batches to prevent food from steaming and becoming soggy.

### 🛡️ Rule 5: The Safety & Allergy Isolation Rule
*   **Order operations:** Always prep allergen-free or vegetarian items *before* handling meat or major allergens (like seafood) to eliminate cross-contamination risk.
*   **Maintain equipment cleanliness:** Schedule a specific 5-minute window in your workflow to clear away dirty prep bowls and sanitize surfaces before moving from Phase 1 (Prep) to Phase 2 (Cooking).

### 🧩 Rule 6: The Multi-Component Rule
*   **Sync components:** Do not start a main component until its dependent component is ready. For example, do not begin cooking steak before steak sauce is finished and available.
*   **Coordinate timing:** Plan dependent components so each element is ready at the right moment, avoiding hold time or overcooking of the primary dish.
