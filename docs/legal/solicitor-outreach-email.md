# Solicitor outreach — ready-to-send email

Copy and paste the body below into a fresh Gmail draft from
`admin@chefflow.uk`. Send individually to 3–5 firms (don't BCC — they'll
all see they're not the only firm being approached, which weakens any
quote you get back).

## Subject line

> Quote request — SaaS Terms + food-allergen liability review (ChefFlow Ltd)

## Body

```
Hi [Partner first name where available, otherwise "team"],

I'm the founder of ChefFlow — a UK-built SaaS used by professional
chefs (private chefs, small bistros, hotel kitchens) to plan recipes,
scale portions, and run service-night workflows. The product is live
at https://chefflow.uk; my legal pages are at /terms and /disclaimer.

I'm looking for a fixed-fee review + amendment of my existing Terms of
Service and Disclaimer, with a specific focus on food-allergen
liability. ChefFlow itself never declares allergens on a chef's behalf
— all allergen tagging is manual, AI-assisted detection has been
removed for risk reasons, and I gate publishing community recipes
behind a clickwrap attestation in which the chef confirms they remain
the food business operator under the Food Information Regulations
2014. The product posture is intentionally defensive; I now want a
specialist to pressure-test the legal language behind it.

Concrete deliverables I'd like:

1. Review my Terms of Service (§13 limitation-of-liability clause —
   currently capped at fees paid or £100). Tell me whether that cap
   would survive challenge under Consumer Rights Act 2015 s.62
   (unfair terms) and Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 against a chef
   whose restaurant could face an unlimited downstream claim. Suggest
   a defensible cap (12 months' subscription fees is the alternative
   I'm considering).

2. Review my Disclaimer (§3 specifically — allergen carve-out). Tell
   me whether it adequately covers cross-contamination, supplier
   reformulation, and ingredient substitution scenarios.

3. Draft an explicit indemnity clause from the publishing chef in
   favour of ChefFlow for any third-party claim arising from a
   community-published recipe. Currently the obligation is implicit
   in the publish-time attestation; I want it explicit.

4. Advise whether ChefFlow should offer a separate B2B variant of the
   Terms (signed agreement) for restaurant-level customers, given
   that B2B contracts allow stronger limitation clauses than the
   consumer-facing default.

5. Written confirmation that operating a SaaS that handles allergen
   data does NOT itself trigger Food Business Operator (FBO)
   registration under the Food Safety Act 1990 / FIR 2014. My read
   is that it doesn't, but I want this in writing.

Optional bonus if scope allows: a one-page incident-response template
for handling an allergen-related complaint (preservation of logs,
communication script, escalation).

Budget guidance: I've ring-fenced £2k–£5k for this matter, with
flexibility if scope expands materially. I'd like a fixed-fee quote
rather than hourly billing where possible.

Timeline: ideally a quote within 2 weeks, scoped engagement complete
within 6 weeks. Not time-critical, but I'd like to be done before
onboarding the first paid restaurant account.

Happy to share my current Terms / Disclaimer PDFs and a 15-minute
walkthrough of the publish flow on Zoom if that helps you scope.

Best,
Derek Shek
Founder, ChefFlow
admin@chefflow.uk
https://chefflow.uk
```

## What to do with the replies

- Most will reply with a quote within 2–5 business days.
- If a firm wants to bill hourly, ask for a "not-to-exceed" cap before
  engaging.
- If a quote is wildly above £5k, ask what's driving the cost — often
  it's because they're assuming a bespoke top-to-bottom rewrite, not
  a targeted review.
- Pick on partner reputation + responsiveness + scope clarity, not
  cheapest price. A solicitor who asks clarifying questions in their
  reply is usually a better engagement than one who quotes blind.
